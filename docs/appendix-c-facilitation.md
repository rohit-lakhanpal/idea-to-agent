How to actually run this with a customer, rather than send it to them.

## The shape of the engagement

| Session | Length | Who | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **0 · Frame the list** | 60–90 min | Whoever assembled the list | A trustworthy list — split, deduplicated, phased |
| **1 · Gate and contract** | Half day | Business owners per area | Stage 1 category + Stage 2 contract for each row |
| **2 · The eight questions** | Half day | Business owners + IT + one risk person | Stages 3, 4 and 5, mostly calculated |
| **3 · Read-back** | 60 min | Sponsor + everyone above | The eight columns, and the list of what is still missing |

**Sessions 1 and 2 can be the same day** for a list under about a dozen rows. Beyond twenty, split by business area rather than trying to do it all at once — attention collapses and the later rows get worse answers.

## Who has to be in the room

- **A business owner per area.** Not a delegate. Stage 2 cannot be answered by anyone else, and a delegate will guess.
- **Someone from IT who knows what connects to what.** Q7 is unanswerable without them, and Q7 is the question that moves rows from *weeks* to *months*.
- **One person from risk, privacy or clinical governance.** Exactly one. They answer Q5, and they will otherwise be the reason the whole thing gets revisited in six weeks.

**Who does not need to be there:** anyone whose contribution is product opinions. The method deliberately defers that, and their presence early pulls every conversation back to tooling.

## Running Stage 0

Put the list on screen and go line by line asking one question: **"is this one job?"**

Expect to grow the list by 30–50 per cent. That feels like going backwards and is the most valuable hour of the engagement.

The tell for a row that needs splitting is a **noun describing a tool** rather than a **verb describing work**. "An HR assistant" splits. "Answer policy questions" does not.

## Running Stage 1

This is where you save the customer the most money, and where they will push back hardest, because *"it's not AI"* sounds like *"we won't help you"*.

Framing that works:

> *"This one's better than an AI project — it's a two-week automation with no governance overhead. Let's get it shipped while we work on the harder ones."*

The counter-question when everything is being called an agent:

> *"Walk me through what happens the second time someone uses this. Is it the same steps?"*

## Running Stage 3

Use **the customer's own examples**, not yours. The eight questions are fast — a row takes about four minutes once people have the rhythm — but only if you resist explaining the framework. Ask the question, take the answer, move on.

**Q1 is the one to fight hardest for.** People will answer "a person asks" reflexively because that is what they have seen. Push: *"Does someone have to remember to go and ask? Or should it come and find them?"*

**Q3: record intended and pilot reach separately.** People conflate them and then feel they have been talked into enterprise scope.

**Q5 is where the room goes quiet.** That is correct and you should let it. If nobody can classify the data, that is the finding.

**Q8b needs an example** or it does not land:

> *"Forget wrong answers for a second. What if it just… stopped, and nobody noticed for a week?"*

## Running the read-back

**Do not present the framework.** Present the eight columns and the list of blanks.

The two lines that do the most work:

> *"These four are quick wins, and here is precisely why they qualify when these others that sound similar do not."*

> *"These blanks are not gaps in our analysis. They are the half of the contract only you can fill, and the rows that come back filled in are the rows with a real owner."*

## Things that reliably go wrong

| What happens | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Someone wants to talk products in session 1 | *"Let's park that — if we answer these eight, the product falls out. If it doesn't, I'll buy you lunch."* |
| The sponsor is not in the room for the read-back | Reschedule. Without them the blanks never get filled. |
| Every row is scored 5 on every lens | Score them **relative to each other**, not absolutely. Force a ranking. |
| A row is genuinely 50/50 | Say so and record it. **The framework's job is to make a judgement call visible, not to hide it in a score.** |
| The value baseline is entirely empty | Expected. Do not fill it with guesses. It becomes the follow-up. |
| Someone challenges *"this is just a Microsoft funnel"* | Point at Stage 1 — a large share of rows leave the AI path entirely — and at *"evaluation is a practice, not a vendor"*. |

## What good looks like afterwards

Not a completed workbook. **A completed workbook is not the goal**, and one that is 100 per cent full after a single workshop probably contains guesses.

What good looks like:

- The list is genuinely decomposed, and everyone agrees the rows are jobs
- Two or three rows are shipping now with light governance
- A couple of rows have been honestly reclassified as programme work
- Every remaining row has a **named person** who owes you specific numbers
- Nobody has bought anything yet
